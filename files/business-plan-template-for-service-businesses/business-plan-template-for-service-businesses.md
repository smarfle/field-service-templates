# Sample business plan
Sample. Fictional business, market assumptions and financial projections. Not a promise of results.

## Summary and company
Sample Coast Cleaning LLC plans a January 2027 launch in Orlando. Avery Reed owns and operates the business and has residential cleaning experience. The business will serve homes within 8 miles of its base, starting with recurring maintenance cleans. Its first-year target is 990 visits and $148,500 of revenue. The owner will fund $12,000 of setup and working cash. Slow customer acquisition is the main risk.

## Services and market
The initial offer averages $150 for one maintenance visit under a written room and task scope. Deep cleaning, interior appliances and move-out jobs need separate pricing. The plan assumes $45 of variable cost per visit, including paid helper time, consumables and incremental travel. Quotes will be tested against actual time before adding customers.

The owner plans interviews with 10 local homeowners and a comparison of 3 nearby cleaners before launch. The working hypothesis is that customers value a consistent crew and clear arrival windows. These are research tasks, not verified market findings. The owner will record dated sources and revise the offer after the interviews.

## Marketing and operations
The initial marketing allowance is $300 monthly, included in the fixed-cost budget. The owner will maintain a Google Business Profile, test local referral outreach and record each inquiry source. The weekly target is 8 qualified inquiries and 3 booked first visits. Results will be reviewed against completed, paid jobs.

Visits run Monday through Friday in nearby clusters, with a travel and rework buffer. The owner manages quotes, scheduling and quality checks. Part-time helper coverage is included in the variable-cost assumption; added permanent staff requires a new cost and capacity review. Before launch, the owner will verify registrations, insurance, safe chemical use, vehicle coverage and worker classification with the relevant advisers.

## Startup funding and risks
| Use of funds | USD |
| :---- | :---- |
| Equipment and vehicle setup | $4,000 |
| Insurance, registration and setup | $1,500 |
| Launch supplies and marketing | $1,000 |

The owner will pause optional marketing and delay hiring if booked visits stay below plan for two months. Equipment failure is a second risk; a local rental supplier will be checked before launch. No loan or grant approval is assumed.

# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy. Set the plan year and purpose, then replace the bracketed prompts. Use the short industry examples only as writing guides, not market evidence.  
2. Draft services, customer type and service radius first. Obtain local price and demand evidence, record sources and check the actual time and direct cost of each service.  
3. Map the process from inquiry to payment. Name owners for follow-up, scheduling, field work, safety and bookkeeping. Verify licenses, insurance and staffing requirements for your trade and location.  
4. List startup purchases and a cash reserve separately from monthly expenses. Explain the funding source and any loan repayments. Do not treat borrowed money or owner funds as sales.  
5. Fill all 12 months manually. Multiply visits by average price, add fixed and variable costs, and check the year totals. Include a low-demand case and a separate cash forecast if customers pay later.  
6. Check break-even against realistic crew capacity. The sample has 990 visits, $148,500 revenue and $92,550 expenses, leaving $55,950 under its assumptions. It is not a tax return, financing approval or guaranteed result.  
7. Set measurable milestones and review actual results monthly. Remove the Sample and usage notes before sharing a plan. The Word and PDF links export the full template. Keep detailed financial and owner records private.
