# Pay and working time
## Pay and timekeeping
Pay periods are [weekly or biweekly] and payday is [day]. Each employee receives their pay rate and overtime classification in writing. Record all time worked in [system], including required meetings, training, loading and travel between job sites during the workday. Record work-related calls and messages outside scheduled hours. Do not work off the clock or ask another person to change a time record.

Submit time by [deadline]. Report a missed punch or pay concern to [contact] promptly. The company will investigate and correct errors. Review travel, on-call and overnight assignments with payroll before the assignment. Required pay is not withheld because a timesheet is late. Lawful deductions and expense reimbursements follow [written policy and state addendum].

## Overtime
Get approval from [manager] before working overtime, except when immediate action is needed for safety. Record the time even if approval was not obtained. The company pays covered nonexempt employees for all overtime required by law. Federal overtime generally applies after 40 hours worked in one workweek at at least one and one-half times the regular rate. More protective state or local rules apply where required. Approval violations may be addressed separately, but do not erase wages owed.

Do not automatically deduct a meal that was worked.

# Safety and field work
## Safety and reporting
Follow training, labels and job-specific safety instructions. Use required protective equipment and stop work if there is an immediate hazard. Notify [safety contact] about injuries, near misses, damaged equipment or unsafe conditions as soon as practical. Call 911 for an emergency. Do not delay medical help to complete a form. Reporting a safety concern or injury in good faith will not lead to retaliation.

Only trained, authorized staff may use chemicals, ladders, powered equipment or regulated trade tools. Never mix chemicals unless the product instructions specifically allow it. Safety data sheets are kept at [location]. Follow [heat, severe weather, electrical, lockout, lifting and lone-worker procedures] that apply to the job. This handbook does not replace required training or a written safety program.

## Vehicles and equipment
Only authorized, properly licensed drivers may operate company vehicles. Inspect vehicles and equipment before use, secure loads, wear seat belts and follow traffic laws. Do not text while driving. Park before handling a device. Report crashes, tickets, damage and license restrictions to [contact]. Personal vehicle use, mileage reimbursement and authorized passengers follow [policy]. No repair-cost deduction from pay is automatic.

## Uniforms and customer homes
Wear [uniform and identification] and the protective equipment for the task. Arrive within the agreed window or notify the office about a delay. Ask before entering private rooms, moving valuables or taking photos. Protect floors and belongings, keep pets and children away from the work area, and secure gates and doors when leaving. Do not smoke, use drugs, solicit personal work or accept unauthorized payments on a customer job. Report damage or a customer complaint to [contact] rather than hiding it.

## Phones and social media
Use [approved work channels] for job details. Protect customer addresses, entry codes, payment data and private records. Do not post customer photos or identifying details without permission. [Insert device security and reimbursement rules.] These rules do not prohibit lawful discussion of wages, working conditions, workplace concerns or other protected activity, including lawful communication with a government agency.

# Concerns and employment changes
## Harassment and complaints
Harassment, discrimination, threats and retaliation are prohibited, including conduct by customers, vendors and supervisors. Report concerns to either listed complaint contact. You may bypass your supervisor, especially if that person is involved. If both contacts are involved, use [independent alternate contact]. You may also contact an appropriate government agency without first using this process.

We will review concerns promptly and fairly, protect information as far as practical, and take appropriate action. Complete secrecy cannot be promised because an investigation may require sharing facts. Employees should cooperate truthfully. Retaliation for a good-faith report, participation in an investigation or exercise of a protected right is prohibited.

## Conduct and discipline
Employees must work honestly, follow lawful safety instructions and treat others respectfully. Theft, falsified records, violence and serious safety breaches may lead to discipline. Depending on the facts and applicable law, action may include coaching, warnings, suspension or termination. No fixed sequence is guaranteed. Discipline must not punish protected leave, wage discussions, safety reports or other lawful protected conduct.

## Leaving the company
Please give [requested notice] when resigning when practical; the request does not change lawful at-will rights. Return keys, devices, tools, uniforms and customer information to [contact]. Final pay, earned leave and any permitted deductions follow the applicable state rules and benefit terms. Do not hold final wages merely until equipment is returned. [Insert the reviewed final-pay and benefit continuation process.]

The company may update policies prospectively, subject to applicable law and binding agreements. Written changes will identify their effective date. State or local addenda control when they provide required protections. Questions go to [contact]. [List attached addenda, safety procedures and benefit documents.]

# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy. Replace every bracketed field and insert the company name consistently.  
2. Have a local employment attorney review at-will language, state addenda, leave, breaks, deductions, final pay and protected employee activity before issuing the handbook.  
3. Fill in a fixed workweek, pay schedule, timekeeping method, overtime contact and expense reimbursement process. Confirm the rules fit each state where employees work.  
4. Name two complaint contacts employees can reach, including a route that bypasses the direct supervisor. Tell staff how to report an urgent safety concern.  
5. Attach job-specific safety procedures and benefit details. Remove choices that do not apply. Do not describe this general handbook as a complete safety program.  
6. Delete the Sample and these template notes from the employee version. Give the completed handbook and addenda to each employee and collect the acknowledgment.  
7. Keep a dated master and signed receipts privately. Review the handbook when laws, locations or company policies change.
