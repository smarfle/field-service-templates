# Working relationship and responsibilities
## Independent contractor status
The parties intend an independent business relationship, not employment. Contractor is responsible for its own business operations and applicable taxes and is not eligible for Client employee benefits solely under this agreement. Contractor may serve other clients and has no authority to bind Client. This label does not determine legal status. The actual work arrangement must meet applicable federal, state and local tests, and nothing waives rights that law gives a worker.

Contractor will provide an appropriate tax form, such as Form W-9, through a secure channel when needed. Client will issue required information returns, including Form 1099-NEC when applicable. Do not place Social Security numbers or tax IDs in a public template or in the general agreement copy.

## Safety and property
Each party is responsible for the safety duties the law places on it. Contractor will use trained personnel and lawful work practices, protect the site and report hazards, injuries or damage promptly to [contact]. Either party may stop work affected by an immediate hazard. No clause transfers a nondelegable legal safety duty or authorizes unlicensed work.

# Information work product and ending the agreement
## Confidentiality
Each party will protect nonpublic customer, access, payment and business information obtained for the work and use it only to perform this agreement. Information already public, independently developed or lawfully received without restriction is excluded. Permitted disclosures include legal process, protected reports and communications with government agencies. Return or securely delete information at the end, except records lawfully retained with continued protection.

## Ownership of work
On payment for the applicable work, Contractor assigns to Client the rights it owns in the deliverables specifically created for Client: [list]. Contractor retains pre-existing tools, methods, templates and general know-how. To the extent those are included in a paid deliverable, Contractor grants Client a nonexclusive license to use them as part of that deliverable for [purpose]. Third-party materials remain subject to their licenses. [Add a separate IP schedule if needed.]

## Term and termination
The agreement runs from [start] to [end or completion], unless ended sooner. Either party may end it on [number] days written notice. A party may end it for a material breach not cured within [number] days after written notice, or immediately for [reviewed serious causes]. Client will pay for accepted work performed and authorized noncancelable costs through termination, less amounts already paid. The parties will arrange return of property and any agreed handoff.

# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy and fill in both legal names, notice contacts and the work details. Do not insert tax IDs into a public or widely shared copy.  
2. Have a local attorney review worker classification before using the agreement. A contract and Form 1099 do not by themselves make someone an independent contractor.  
3. Describe measurable services and exclusions. Distinguish a completion deadline or customer access window from employee-style control of daily work.  
4. Set the rate or fixed price, invoice timing, payment deadline, dispute process and expense approval. Verify licenses and insurance needed for the actual services.  
5. Choose ownership and confidentiality terms that fit the deliverables. Identify any pre-existing or third-party materials and required licenses.  
6. Complete termination, dispute and state-law fields. Delete choices you do not use, remove the Sample and template notes, and attach any agreed schedules.  
7. Both parties sign the same completed version and each keeps a copy. Put later scope or price changes in a written amendment before work changes.
