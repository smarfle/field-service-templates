# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy. Enter the business, customer and job address, then assign an estimate number, issue date and valid-until date.  
2. Describe the scope before pricing it. State the equipment, areas, quantities or visit frequency so the customer can compare the price with the actual work offered.  
3. Multiply each quantity by its rate for the line amount. Add the line amounts for the subtotal and apply only tax that is due for your work. This document does not calculate totals automatically.  
4. State the deposit as an amount or a percentage of the total and explain when it is due. The deposit is part of the total, not an extra charge. In the sample, $215 plus $15.05 tax is $230.05. A 25% deposit rounds to $57.51, leaving $172.54.  
5. Write assumptions, exclusions, payment timing and proposed schedule. Confirm local rules on deposits, required service or home-improvement contracts, notices and tax. The sample tax rate and deposit are examples, not advice.  
6. Ask the customer to review and sign the final version before authorizing the work. Confirm the start date separately. Any later change to scope or price should be approved in writing.  
7. Delete the Sample and notes from the customer version. The top Word and PDF links download the full template. Print only the estimate page to PDF if needed, check that it fits after editing and retain the accepted version privately.
