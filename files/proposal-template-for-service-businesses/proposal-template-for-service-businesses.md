# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy. Fill the cover with the customer, project, business contact, proposal number, issue date and validity date. Keep private access information out of the document.  
2. Use the customer’s stated needs and a site visit or call to define scope. Replace generic promises with tasks, areas, quantities and a way to check completion. Support experience and credential claims.  
3. Set a timeline that reflects crew capacity, access and approvals. Explain what is included and excluded before comparing pricing options.  
4. Offer Good, Better and Best only when each has a real scope difference. State whether prices are one-time or recurring. Select one, calculate applicable tax and complete the total and payment fields manually.  
5. Review warranty, cancellation, renewal and other terms with local counsel where needed. Attach any required service agreement and notices. Do not rely on this proposal to replace state-specific contract requirements.  
6. Ask the customer to confirm the selected scope and price, then sign the same version. Confirm the start date separately. In the sample, Better is $950 per month, not $800 + $950 + $1,200.  
7. Remove the Sample and usage notes before sending. The Word and PDF links export the full template. Check page breaks after editing and retain the signed proposal and attachments privately.
