Customer and Provider must agree in writing to changes in scope, materials, price or schedule before extra work starts. Discovery of unsafe conditions or hidden damage may require a pause and a revised scope. Emergency authorization, if any, is [process and spending cap].

# How to use this template
1. Make a private copy and enter both parties, the service location and notice contacts. Never leave entry codes in the public master.  
2. Have a local attorney review the contract, consumer cancellation requirements, warranty, liability and any renewal language before use. Add all legally required notices and forms.  
3. Keep the relevant service schedule and delete the others. Fill in scope, frequency, price and exclusions so neither party has to guess what a visit includes.  
4. Set invoice timing, deposit and refund treatment, missed-visit rules, the term and how to end service. Use written renewal consent unless a separately reviewed renewal arrangement is needed.  
5. Confirm access, pets, hazards, utilities, supplies and job-specific licensing. Define how weather or equipment findings change the schedule.  
6. Remove the Sample and template notes. Both parties sign the main agreement and initial each retained schedule. Give each party the complete signed version.  
7. Use a written change order for later scope or price changes. Save service records, invoices and notices with the private customer file.
